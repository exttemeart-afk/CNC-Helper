CNC Lathe Turning Speeds, Feeds & Tool Nose Theory

1. Cutting Speed Vc and Spindle RPM n

Surface cutting speed Vc (m/min) represents the peripheral speed of the rotating workpiece:

Vc = (π × Dw × n) / 1000 [m/min]  |  n = (1000 × Vc) / (π × Dw) [RPM]

CNC lathes typically run in Constant Surface Speed (CSS) mode using G96 S..., paired with a maximum spindle speed clamp G50 S... (Fanuc/Haas) or LIMS=... (Siemens) to prevent centrifugal jaw gripping force loss when facing toward center (X = 0).

2. Theoretical Ra and Rz Surface Finish

Theoretical finish is governed by feed per revolution f (mm/rev) and insert corner radius r (mm):

  • Peak-to-valley profile height (Rz): Rz = (f² / (8 × r)) × 1000 [µm]
  • Centerline average roughness (Ra): Ra = (f² / (31.2 × r)) × 1000 [µm]

Machinist Rule of Thumb: For an r = 0.8 mm insert at f = 0.2 mm/rev, theoretical roughness is Ra ≈ 1.6 µm (Rz ≈ 6.25 µm). Halving feed to 0.1 mm/rev drops Ra to ≈ 0.4 µm.

3. Manual Tool Nose Radius Compensation (TNRC)

When turning tapers or 45° chamfers without CNC radius compensation (G41/G42), the insert tool nose radius shifts the actual cutting edge. For a 45° chamfer:

  • Z-axis shift: ΔZ = r × (1 - tan(22.5°)) ≈ 0.414 × r
  • X-axis diameter shift: ΔX = 2 × r × (1 - tan(22.5°)) ≈ 0.828 × r
